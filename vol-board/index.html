What each setting does · how to use this board
IV Term
Which implied vol drives the quadrant up/down axis (IV z-score), the σ moves and carry. IV30 for trades out to about 6 weeks, IV60 or IV90 for longer ones.
Δ Vol
How far back the change in IV30 looks in the Metrics table: 1 day or 5 days. Positive means options got more expensive.
Δ Skew
How far back the change in skew looks (1, 5 or 10 days). Drives the Δ Skew chart and the Metrics table. Rising skew means puts are getting bid.
Carry vs
Which realized vol IV is compared with. HV10 reacts fastest to recent moves, HV30 is steadier. Carry above 1× means options price more movement than the market has delivered.
Cadence
DAILY shows today's move in the return strips and σ bars. WEEKLY shows the past week.
Group
Narrows every chart and the table to one group (for example Bonds only). ALL shows everything.
Quadrant spot axis
The dropdown in the Regime Quadrant header. BB %: where price sits in its 20-day Bollinger bands, 0% = lower band (left dashed line), 50% = middle, 100% = upper band (right dashed line). 1W σ: this week's move measured against what options priced in.
How to use it
  1. Check the gauges at the top are green: fresh data comes after the 9:30 AM open.
  2. Set IV Term to match how long you would hold the trade.
  3. On the Regime Quadrant, look for tickers far from the center box. Top half = implied vol rich, so selling premium pays; bottom half = vol cheap, so buying options is cheap. Left = price near the lower band; right = near the upper band. The quadrant label is the trade idea; extreme bands are the stronger readings, the center box means use a spread.
  4. Confirm with the other charts: Skew (are puts expensive?), Term structure (above 100 = event or stress priced in), Carry (IV rich vs realized?).
  5. Click any ticker for its drill-down: the read in plain words, every metric ranked, and open interest by strike.
  6. Italic tickers use a stand-in z-score until 20 days of history build up. The quadrant screens for mispriced premium; it is not a trade signal by itself.

  • Cross-Asset Regime and Sectors: open "What each setting does" under the settings bar. Check the gauges are green, set IV Term to your holding period, then read the Regime Quadrant: top = vol rich (sell premium), bottom = vol cheap (buy options), left = near the lower Bollinger band, right = near the upper band.   • VIX Term & Carry: open "How to use the VIX Term & Carry" at the top of that screen. Start with the futures curve (contango = calm, inverted = stress), then roll-down, VVIX/VIX and beta.
  • Vol Surface Δ: open "How to use Vol Surface Δ" at the top of that screen. Pick a symbol and a Date 2 offset, then read where on the smile vol moved.

How the data gets hereFeeds

Cboe delayed chains→KGR data engine, every 15 min→this page
Cboe delayed quotes + daily price history→same worker→PRICES table

Options numbers come from Cboe's delayed quote feed, about 15 minutes behind. The KGR data engine pulls each ETF's full chain, plus the VIX option chain, on weekdays from 9:30 to 4:15 ET. It works out IV30, IV60, IV90, 25-delta skew, term structure, percentiles and open interest and stores the SNAPSHOT, HISTORY, OI, VIX_SUM and VIX_OI tables. This page reads them every 5 minutes.

The VIX Term & Carry runs on Cboe too: the worker pulls 15-minute delayed VIX, VVIX and SPX index quotes every run, and once a day the Cboe daily history for VIX, VVIX and SPX plus the settlement file for every VIX futures contract, which builds UX1, UX2 and the curve. Prices on the Macro and Sectors boards are Cboe 15-minute delayed quotes. Returns, Bollinger bands and HV10/20/30 are computed in the worker from daily closes (today's bar set to the Cboe last) and written to the PRICES tab. Everything the page shows comes from the worker, so its 15-minute refresh is the one clock for the whole page.

Every screen shows a timestamp line under the title: when the page last received data, and the Cboe stamp on each feed. Cboe quotes and chains are 15 minutes delayed; VIX futures use the daily settlement, so the curve updates after each close.

The gauges at the top show how fresh each feed is. The Cboe gauge measures the age of the Cboe timestamp on the latest chain: green under 20 minutes, amber to 45, red beyond, grey when the market is closed. Coverage counts tickers that returned a chain. History counts the days stored per ticker; z-scores and percentiles switch on at 20 days.

GlossaryEvery term on the board

IV30 · IV60 · IV90Implied vol
At-the-money implied volatility at 30, 60 and 90 calendar days, annualized, in %. Read off each expiry's out-of-the-money smile at spot, then interpolated between expiries in total variance.The market's price for movement. IV30 of 20 means options price roughly a 20% one-standard-deviation move over a year.
ATM IVImplied vol
Implied vol at the strike equal to spot. The board uses puts below spot and calls above, tight two-sided quotes first, so one stale quote can't drive it.The anchor every other vol number is compared with.
25Δ put IV · 25Δ call IVImplied vol
Implied vol of the 25-delta put and 25-delta call at 30 days. Delta is roughly the option's chance of finishing in the money, so 25Δ options are about 1-in-4 bets.The wings: what protection and upside cost.
SkewSkew
25Δ put IV minus 25Δ call IV at 30 days, in vol points.Positive is normal for equities. Higher means puts are bid; negative means calls are bid, common in commodities.
Skew zSkew
Today's skew against the instrument's own skew history, in standard deviations.Above +1 puts are expensive versus normal; below −1 protection is cheap.
Δ skewSkew
Change in skew over the Δ Skew window (1D, 5D or 10D), in vol points or in z units.Rising skew after a selloff is the market paying up for more protection.
Div?Skew
✓ when skew moved the same way as the 1-week return.Unusual for equities, which normally see skew rise on selloffs. A rally with rising skew is a hedged rally.
IV z-scoreStatistics
(today's IV − average) ÷ standard deviation, over up to 252 stored days. Switches on at 20 days.Above +1 vol is rich versus its own past, below −1 cheap. ±2 is an extreme.
IV %-ileStatistics
Share of stored days with IV30 at or below today.90 means vol is higher than on 90% of stored days. Easier to read than z when the history is skewed.
Stand-in zStatistics
Used until a ticker has 20 days stored. IV uses the cross-sectional z of ln(IV ÷ HV20) across the board today; skew uses the cross-sectional z of skew ÷ IV. Shown in italics.Ranks tickers against each other today, not against their own past.
Term structure (T/S)Term
IV30 ÷ IV90 × 100.Under 100 is contango, the normal state. Over 100 is inverted: near-term options cost more than longer ones, which happens around events or stress.
ContangoTerm
Longer-dated vol or futures priced above near-dated.The calm-market shape. Short-vol positions earn the roll.
BackwardationTerm
Near-dated vol or futures priced above longer-dated.Stress or an event close by. Short-vol carry turns negative.
HV10 · HV20 · HV30Realized vol
Realized (historical) volatility: standard deviation of daily log returns over 10, 20 or 30 sessions, × √252, in %.What the instrument actually delivered. Compare with IV.
Carry (IV ÷ HV)Realized vol
Implied vol divided by realized vol over the Carry vs window. The Metrics table shows it as (IV − HV) ÷ IV in %.Above 1 (or positive %), options price more movement than delivered, which favors selling premium. Below 1 favors owning options.
Vol risk premiumRealized vol
The gap between implied and later-realized vol. Usually positive, because hedgers pay for protection.The edge premium sellers collect on average, and lose in crashes.
σ moveMoves
Return divided by the move options priced. Daily implied move = IV ÷ √252; weekly = IV ÷ √52.1.5σ means the instrument moved one and a half times what options expected. Whole groups moving 1σ+ the same way usually means rotation.
Implied moveMoves
Spot × IV × √(days ÷ 365), the one-standard-deviation move options price to an expiry.About two thirds of outcomes should land inside it.
BB %BPrice
Where price sits in its 20-day, 2σ Bollinger bands: 0 the lower band, 0.5 the middle, 1 the upper. Boll % is %B × 100.Under 0 or over 1 is stretched outside the bands.
Regime QuadrantSignals
IV z on the vertical axis against how stretched spot is (1W σ or BB %B) on the horizontal.Rich IV + spot down: sell put. Rich IV + spot up: sell call. Cheap IV + spot down: buy call. Cheap IV + spot up: buy put. Inner ring is the spread zone with little edge. A screen, not a trade signal.
Open interest (OI)Positioning
Contracts outstanding at the close, from Cboe.Where positions sit. Large call OI above spot or UX1 marks where hedges and overwrites are.
P/CPositioning
Put volume or OI ÷ call volume or OI.Above 1 puts dominate. Extremes often mark crowded hedging, not direction.
VIXVIX
Cboe's 30-day implied vol index for the S&P 500, from SPX options.The equity market's fear gauge. Spot VIX itself can't be traded.
UX1 · UX2VIX
The front and second monthly VIX futures, from Cboe daily settlements.What VIX ETPs and hedgers actually hold.
Roll-downVIX
(UX1 − VIX) ÷ UX1.Positive in contango: what a short UX1 position earns if VIX holds still into expiry. Negative in backwardation.
VVIXVIX
The implied vol of VIX options, Cboe's vol of vol.High VVIX means VIX calls are expensive.
VVIX/VIXVIX
VVIX divided by VIX.Rising while VIX stays low means tail hedges are getting expensive relative to the level of vol.
β to SPXVIX
Least-squares slope of UX1 % change on SPX % change, over 3 months or a rolling 20 sessions. R² is the fit quality.More negative means VIX futures react harder to equity moves, a stronger hedge.
Vol surfaceSurface
Implied vol by delta (5Δ put through ATM to 5Δ call) for each expiry out to 130 days.Shape of the smile and term in one picture.
Δ IV by deltaSurface
Change in vol points at each delta between two dates (1d, 2d, 5d or 10d).Where on the smile the repricing happened: wings, ATM, or the whole curve.
Forward volSurface
Vol implied between two expiries: √((σ₂²t₂ − σ₁²t₁) ÷ (t₂ − t₁)).Isolates the vol priced for one window, such as the week of an event.
15-min delayedData
Cboe's free quotes run about 15 minutes behind the market.Fine for regime reads; not for execution.
Cboe stampData
The time Cboe stamped on the chain or quote it served, in UTC.If it's far behind the fetch time, Cboe hasn't updated that ticker (common for thin ETFs).

Reading the numbersRules of thumb

|z| under 1
Normal. No read from that metric alone.
|z| 1 to 2
Worth a look. Rich or cheap versus its own history.
|z| over 2
Extreme. Check the drill-down and the Cboe stamp before trusting it; thin ETFs can print a bad quote.
T/S over 100
Front vol above back vol. Look for an event on the strip at the top or stress in the group.
Carry over 1.2
Options price at least 20% more movement than delivered. Premium-selling territory if the regime is calm.
Carry under 0.9
Realized is running hotter than implied. Owning options is cheap relative to movement.
σ move over ±1.5
A move the options market didn't expect. One name is news; a whole group is rotation.
Roll-down over 5%
Steep VIX contango: short-vol carry is rich. Under 0 is backwardation.

QuestionsFAQ

Why "sample data"?
The data feed hasn't answered yet. The page shows illustrative values until it does, and says so in the banner.
How fresh is it?
The worker runs every 15 minutes on weekdays, 9:30 to 4:15 ET. Cboe quotes are 15 minutes delayed, so numbers are 15 to 30 minutes old. VIX futures use the daily settlement.
Why are z-scores blank?
They need 20 stored days per ticker. Until then the board uses stand-ins, shown in italics.
A number looks wrong
Open the ticker's drill-down and check the Cboe stamp. Thin ETFs sometimes have no fresh quote; the worker skips wide markets and single bad quotes, and the next run usually fixes it.
Weekends and holidays
No runs. The board shows Friday's last values.
Who sees that I visited?
Only the board’s owner. Each time you open the board (at most once per 30 minutes) it logs your sign-in email, the time and which screen was open. Nothing else is recorded.
Is this advice?
No. It's a screen of public delayed market data for information only, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

Return stripsTop rows

Each instrument's price change for the cadence window, by group. Green is up, red is down. Click a ticker for its drill-down.

σ move barsThree panels

The return in standard deviations of the move the options market priced in. The daily implied move is IV ÷ √252 and the weekly one is IV ÷ √52. A 1.5σ bar means the instrument moved one and a half times what options expected. Large σ moves in one direction across a whole group usually mean rotation, not single-name news.

Regime QuadrantMain panel

The vertical axis is IV z-score: up means implied vol is rich against its history, down means cheap. The horizontal axis is how stretched spot is, and the selector in the panel header switches it:

  • 1W σ: the 1-week return in implied-move units. Left of center has sold off, right has run up.
  • BB %B: where price sits in its 20-day, 2σ Bollinger bands. %B 0 is the lower band at the left ring edge, 0.5 the middle band at center, 1 the upper band at the right ring edge.

Rich IV with spot stretched down reads sell put; rich IV with spot up reads sell call; cheap IV with spot down reads buy call; cheap IV with spot up reads buy put. The center box is the spread zone, where the edge is small. The middle and extreme bands are stronger readings, and anything outside the rings is an extreme on at least one axis. It screens for mispriced premium; it is not a trade signal by itself.

Skew z-score vs IV z-scoreScatter

Skew is 25-delta put IV minus 25-delta call IV at 30 days, in vol points, compared with the instrument's own history. Right of zero, puts are bid relative to normal; left, puts are cheap. Top right means both overall vol and downside protection are expensive.

Term structure vs IV z-scoreScatter

IV30 ÷ IV90 as a percent. Under 100 is contango, the normal state. Over 100 is inverted: near-term options priced above longer ones, which happens around events or stress.

Δ skew vs 1-week σ moveScatter

Change in skew z-score over the Δ Skew window against the 1-week σ move. Skew rising after a selloff is the market paying up for more protection. It needs 20 days of history plus the window, so it stays empty for the first few weeks.

Carry vs HVScatter

Implied vol divided by realized vol. Above 1, options price more movement than the instrument has delivered, which favors selling premium. Below 1 favors owning options.

Until an instrument has 20 days stored, IV z uses a cross-sectional stand-in: the z-score of ln(IV ÷ HV20) across the board today. Skew z uses the cross-sectional z of skew ÷ IV. Stand-ins show in italics on the charts. They rank instruments against each other rather than against their own past.

Vol Surface ΔSurface

Implied vol by delta (5-delta put through ATM to 5-delta call) for each expiry, for ^SPX, GLD, IWM, QQQ and TLT. The worker builds it from Cboe's 15-minute delayed chains every 30 minutes, using Fridays and month-end expiries out to 130 days. Each delta bucket is interpolated from Cboe's per-option IV and delta; ATM is interpolated at spot.

Date 2 at 1d is the previous close. The worker backs implied vol out of each option's previous-close price (Black-Scholes, 4% rate, previous spot from put-call parity on those same closes), so day-over-day changes work from day one. 2d, 5d and 10d use the surface history, which stores one snapshot per session and keeps 12.

The table shows the surface for the chosen expiries next to the change in vol points, green where vol rose. The surface and change charts cover every stored expiry. Vol skew plots one expiry live against date 2 with the change as bars. Term structure plots ATM vol by expiry, forward vol between expiries, a fitted line, the expected move to each expiry (ATM × √(days/365)) and the macro events.
